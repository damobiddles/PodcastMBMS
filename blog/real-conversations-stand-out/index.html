When AI makes the content, real conversations stand out

Almost every marketing team now uses AI to make content. Shoppers are asking AI to find products for them, and search engines answer questions before anyone clicks a link. So the thing that’s getting harder to find is a real person saying something worth hearing.

We’ve read the latest research from Kantar, HubSpot, the DMA and the trade press to pull out what’s changing, what it means for a business like yours, and why a good conversation fits so neatly into all of it.

1. Everyone is using AI, so human content stands out

In HubSpot’s 2026 State of Marketing report, a survey of more than 1,500 marketers, about four in five said they use AI for content creation (42.5% extensively and 38% occasionally). In the same survey, 62.7% said marketers need more unique, human-centred content to compete with AI content.

Listeners feel it too. In Edison Research’s UK Podcast Consumer 2026 study, 72% of weekly podcast listeners in the UK said they see AI as a threat to the credibility of podcasts. People care whether a voice is real.

What it means for you: use AI for the repetitive work, by all means. But the parts people remember, such as opinions, stories and expertise, need to come from real people in your business.

2. Answers are replacing search results

HubSpot’s report says half of consumers now use AI-powered search, and more than a third of marketers (37.7%) plan to increase their investment in AI chatbots like ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude next year. Kantar’s Marketing Trends 2026 calls the work Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO): making sure your brand is known and clearly described, so AI models can recommend it. You’ll also hear it called answer engine optimisation (AEO).

What it means for you: AI answers are built from what’s already written about you. Clear pages in your own words, real expertise and mentions in places people trust all help.

3. Shoppers are sending AI to do the research

Kantar found that 24% of AI users already use an AI shopping assistant, and 74% of people who use AI assistants regularly look for AI-driven recommendations. Its first trend for 2026 is about brands learning to serve these “non-human shoppers” as well as people.

What it means for you: make the facts about what you sell easy to read: what it is, who it’s for, what it costs, and what real customers say.

4. Creators and communities, with a catch

Kantar says a net 61% of marketers plan to spend more on creator content in 2026, but only 27% of creator content ties strongly to the brand behind it. Its advice is long-term creative platforms rather than one-off posts. Kantar also sees people moving away from algorithm-driven feeds, which tend to reward generic, salesy content, and towards smaller communities where relevance matters more than reach.

What it means for you: work with people who genuinely fit your brand, keep doing it over time, and measure more than likes.

5. Ads are turning up in new places, and as entertainment

Retail media, where shops sell ad space on their own sites and apps, keeps growing: Kantar says a net 35% of marketers plan to increase their investment in 2026. It’s spreading beyond retail too. In November 2026, British Airways owner IAG launched IAG Media, which lets advertisers target travellers by destination and cabin class on inflight wifi login pages, inflight entertainment, apps and boarding passes, across British Airways, Iberia, Aer Lingus, Vueling and LEVEL.

Some brands are making the show instead of interrupting it. L’Oréal’s Urban Decay made a six-episode microdrama, Ghost Hunter Hotline, with the short-drama platform ReelShort. L’Oréal USA’s chief digital and marketing officer Han Wen told Marketing Dive that you should “think about entertainment first as the primary purpose”.

6. B2B marketing is getting more effective

The DMA’s report The Value of B2B Marketing 2026, reported by Decision Marketing, looked at 303 B2B entries to the DMA Awards. The average number of business effects each campaign reported rose by 70% in two years, overtaking B2C campaigns for the first time. That’s award entries rather than every campaign, but it’s a clear sign that B2B marketers are measuring, and proving, more.

Why podcasts fit so well

Look at those trends together and a pattern shows up: people want real voices, AI tools want clear expert content, communities want something worth following, and brands want to entertain rather than interrupt. A good podcast does all four at once.

  • It’s hard to fake. A real conversation with real people is exactly the human-led content that stands out when so much else is automated.
  • It gives you words worth finding. One episode’s transcript holds your opinions and expertise in your own words, ready to become articles, FAQs and posts that people and AI tools can find.
  • It builds a community. Listeners choose to subscribe, which is the opposite of being pushed something by an algorithm. And it’s not just audio now: Edison found 72% of UK weekly podcast listeners also watch video podcasts, and YouTube has overtaken Spotify as their most-used service.
  • It’s branded entertainment you own. Long before microdramas, a podcast was a show made by a brand that people watch because they want to.
  • It works with creators who fit. Your guests are experts and voices chosen because they suit your brand, so what they say ties back to you.
  • It suits B2B. Long, thoughtful conversations fit long, considered buying decisions. We’ve written more about how B2B companies turn a podcast into a pipeline.

A podcast on its own isn’t the answer, though. The value comes from what you make from each episode, and from getting it in front of the right people. That’s where content running through everything comes in.

Five things to do this quarter

  • Ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity what they know about your business, and fix what’s wrong or missing
  • Make the facts about what you sell clear, current and easy to find
  • Put a real person from your business front and centre
  • Choose creators and guests who genuinely fit, and work with them for longer
  • Measure what matters to the business, not just likes and views

Questions about these marketing trends

What is answer engine optimisation (AEO)?

It’s making sure your business shows up, and is described accurately, in the answers AI tools like ChatGPT, Gemini and Google’s AI summaries give. Kantar calls it Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO). It comes down to clear, expert content in your own words and being mentioned in places people trust.

Does a podcast help with AI search?

It can. Each episode gives you a transcript full of your expertise in your own words, which you can turn into articles, FAQs and posts. Guests and the places they share the episode can bring mentions too. The podcast itself is the start: the written content you make from it is what search and AI tools read.

Do I need a podcast to keep up?

No. Any regular source of real conversation helps: interviews, webinars, talks or a founder’s video. A podcast is simply one of the best ways we know to make that a habit, and to get a month of content from each conversation.
