How much does it cost to make a podcast? Marlow, the Thames Valley and London

"How much will a podcast cost?" is usually the first question we're asked, and the honest answer is: it depends on what you want it to do.

A simple audio conversation with a colleague costs very little to produce. A multi-camera video show with clips for every social channel costs more, but it also does a lot more work for your business. This guide breaks down where the money goes, how recording locally in Marlow and the Thames Valley compares with booking a London studio, and what a typical episode costs in practice.

All prices here are our own guide prices. They're a starting point rather than a fixed rate, and we're always happy to build something around your budget.

Recording locally or in a London studio?

We're based in Marlow, Buckinghamshire, and we record in three ways. Each suits a different kind of show.

On location in Marlow and the Thames Valley

We bring our mobile studio, built around the RØDECaster Pro II, to your office, event or chosen venue, and run the session just as we would in a studio. Nobody has to travel into London, your team can step out of a meeting and straight into the recording, and the setting can become part of the story.

The trade-off is the room. Offices and venues aren't designed for sound, so we spend time choosing the quietest workable space and setting up carefully. We wrote about what that involves in A podcast on the mats.

In a London studio

For a polished, multi-camera look, or when your guests are based in London, we record at venues such as Hale House. The room is acoustically treated, the set is designed for video, and everything is ready before you arrive. You'll need to factor in travel time for anyone coming in from outside London.

Remotely, wherever your guests are

When guests can't be in the same room, we record with Riverside. Each person is recorded in high quality on their own device, so it works for guests anywhere in the world. It's often the most cost-effective option for interview shows, and you can read how the tools compare in Riverside vs Descript.

What a typical episode costs

Here's how the building blocks add up for three common types of show, using our guide prices:

  • A simple audio show: from around £520 per episode. A recording session (£250), audio editing (from £175) and show notes (from £95). Ideal for a regular conversation between colleagues or with a guest.
  • A video podcast for YouTube and LinkedIn: from around £890 per episode. A recording session (£250), audio and video editing (from £350), show notes (from £95) and a pack of five captioned clips (from £195).
  • A complete, hands-off episode: from around £950. Our complete package covers recording, editing, show notes, distribution and three social clips, so you just turn up and talk.

Recording two episodes in one 90-minute session is a simple way to bring the cost per episode down, and regular clients can move to a monthly package built around how often they publish.

What pushes the price up or down

  • How many people are recording, and how many cameras you want
  • Whether you need video, or audio is enough
  • How long each episode runs, and how much editing it needs
  • How many clips, posts and articles you want from each recording
  • How often you publish, and whether you record several episodes in one session
  • Where you'd like to record, and how far we need to travel

Could you do it yourself?

You can. A couple of decent microphones, a recording tool and an editing app will get you started, and plenty of good shows began that way. The real cost is time: setting up, checking levels, editing, writing show notes, cutting clips and publishing every episode. For most businesses, that time is better spent on the conversation itself and on putting the finished episodes to work.
