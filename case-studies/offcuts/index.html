Our own work · Product

One episode in, a month of posts out

Offcuts and Posting Desk are the two tools we built to do it. Offcuts makes the posts. Posting Desk gets them posted. Both are set up around each show, and a person checks every clip and caption before anything goes out.

Book a call See it step by step

Offcuts

Makes the posts

Offcuts runs on our Mac, so the episode video never leaves it. We give it the episode, a music track, the show’s brand kit and the guest’s name and job. It does the rest in four steps.

Transcribe

The words, the timing of every word and who is speaking when, so captions land on the beat and the frame can follow the person talking.

Pick clips

Claude reads the transcript and picks 30 to 60 second moments that stand on their own, then writes a caption for each channel.

Review

We watch every clip in the show’s look, choose how it’s framed, set the music by ear, edit the captions and dates, and approve. Nothing is made without that tick.

Make

Clips in portrait and square, a cover for each in the show’s colours, every caption, and one zip with the lot.

Posting Desk

Gets them posted

When an episode is finished, one button in Offcuts uploads it to Posting Desk and gives it a private link. We send that link to whoever posts: us, an intern or the client’s own team.

No login, no zip

They open the link and everything is there: each day’s clips with the video, cover and caption for TikTok, Instagram, YouTube Shorts and LinkedIn.

Ready to copy

Copy the caption, save the video and cover, and open the right app’s upload page, all from the card.

Ticked off by name

Each post is posted or scheduled by a person, who ticks Posted. Everyone sees the tick and who made it, so it’s clear what’s out and what’s left.

One episode per link

Each link shows only its own episode. Only we can add or remove episodes, and a link can be replaced if it’s shared too widely.

Why we built our own

Made for your show, not for everyone

  • Your look on every clip. Each show has its own brand kit: logo, background and caption colours, and the show name on the covers.
  • Your voice in every caption. The kit tells Claude what the show is about and who hosts it, and Claude sees captions from that show’s earlier episodes as a style guide, never another client’s.
  • Names spelled right. Each kit keeps a list of the names and terms the transcriber tends to mishear, and fixes them every time.
  • Framed by a person. Fill the frame and drag it, keep the whole shot, or follow whoever is speaking in a two-person conversation.
  • Checked before it’s made. Every claim, name and job title is read on the review page. For a health show, that includes a line saying it’s general information, not medical advice.
  • Posted by a person. Nothing goes out automatically. Posting Desk makes posting quick, and someone still chooses the moment.
